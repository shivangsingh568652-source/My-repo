# Minute Momentum — YouTube Shorts launch kit

A ready-to-customize starter kit for a short-form YouTube channel about practical productivity. It includes a channel blueprint, starter scripts, and a 30-day publishing plan.

> **YouTube calls its short-form videos Shorts.** “Reels” is Instagram’s term. This repository prepares the channel and content; it cannot create or configure a YouTube account for you. You’ll need to create the channel while signed in to your Google account and check that the name/handle are available.

## Channel concept

**Minute Momentum** shares one small, practical productivity idea per short. Each video aims to be useful, clear, and doable—not a promise of instant transformation.

- **Audience:** students, early-career professionals, and busy people who want manageable ways to focus and organize their day.
- **Format:** vertical 9:16 video, generally 20–40 seconds, with captions and one takeaway.
- **Voice:** encouraging, direct, curious; avoid shame, hype, and unsupported claims.
- **Starting cadence:** 3 Shorts each week for 30 days. Adjust based on the time you can sustain.

The name and positioning are a starting point. Check name/handle availability and tailor the niche before publishing.

## Get started

1. Sign in to YouTube with the Google account you want to use, create a channel, and choose a channel name and handle.
2. Add a profile image and banner. See [`docs/channel-blueprint.md`](docs/channel-blueprint.md) for suggested copy and dimensions to verify in YouTube Studio.
3. Pick one idea from [`content/30-day-plan.csv`](content/30-day-plan.csv) and draft it using [`templates/shorts-script.md`](templates/shorts-script.md).
4. Record vertically, add readable captions, and check that music and footage are yours or properly licensed.
5. Upload as a Short. Confirm current eligibility, duration, and upload requirements in YouTube Studio, since platform rules can change.
6. Review retention, comments, and which topics viewers respond to. Use those signals to shape the next batch; don’t judge the channel from one upload.

## Before publishing

- Verify the channel name and handle are available.
- Check every factual claim and cite sources in the description when appropriate.
- Use original or licensed visuals/audio; get permission before featuring someone else.
- Avoid misleading titles, fabricated results, and engagement bait.
- Recheck YouTube’s current policies and upload guidance.
